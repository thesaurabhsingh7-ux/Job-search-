# Outreach guide

This is for reaching out to people at the brands/agencies your job-finder
tracks — done manually, by you, since that's both safer for your LinkedIn
account and more effective than anything automated. Cold outreach that's
clearly personalized gets read; anything that looks automated gets ignored
or reported.

## Who to search for

On LinkedIn, use the search bar with `<Company Name>` + one of these titles.
Roughly in order of how likely they are to actually respond:

1. **"Talent Acquisition" / "Recruiter" / "HR"** — fastest replies, but least
   likely to remember you later. Good for "is this role still open" questions.
2. **"Marketing Manager" / "Brand Manager" / "Growth Manager"** at the company
   — the person who'd actually be your future manager or peer. Best target
   for a genuine, specific message.
3. **Founder / Co-founder** — only for smaller D2C brands (the ones in the
   "smaller / early-stage" section of the career-page list). At a 15-person
   startup, founders often personally read marketing applications; at Nykaa
   or Honasa this is a waste of a message.
4. **"Head of Marketing" / "VP Marketing" / "CMO"** — highest-value contact
   but lowest response rate unless your message gives them a specific reason
   to care.

Filter LinkedIn search results by "2nd" or "3rd+" connections and look for
people who post — active posters are more likely to see and respond to a
connection request message.

## Message templates

Keep these under 300 characters if sending as a LinkedIn connection request
note (LinkedIn's limit); longer is fine once you're already connected or
sending an InMail/email.

### Template 1 — To a Marketing/Brand Manager (peer-level, specific ask)

> Hi [Name] — saw you lead marketing at [Company]. I've run influencer
> campaigns for D2C brands like Mamaearth and boAt for 5 years (one hit 8M+
> views and 3x'd sales for a haircare client), and [Company]'s [something
> specific — a recent campaign, product launch, category] caught my eye.
> Would love to connect and hear how the team approaches influencer/content
> work — open to a quick chat if you have 10 minutes sometime.

### Template 2 — To a Founder (smaller/early-stage brands)

> Hi [Name] — following [Company]'s growth in [category]. I've spent 5 years
> running influencer campaigns for D2C brands (Nykd, Mamaearth, boAt among
> them) and also build my own web products end-to-end, which is a bit
> unusual for a marketing background. If you're building out marketing at
> [Company], I'd love to trade notes — happy to share what's worked for
> similar-stage brands.

### Template 3 — To Talent/HR (direct role inquiry)

> Hi [Name] — I came across [Company]'s marketing team and wanted to check
> if you're currently hiring for influencer marketing or content roles. I
> run SS Media, a 5-year influencer marketing practice with D2C clients
> including Mamaearth, boAt, and WOW Skincare — happy to share my CV if
> there's a fit.

### Template 4 — Warm follow-up (after they accept/reply)

> Thanks for connecting! To give more context — my strongest result was a
> 100+ micro-influencer campaign for a haircare brand that hit 8M+ views and
> drove 3x sales growth. I'm currently looking for a full-time
> influencer/content marketing role and [Company] stood out — would you be
> open to a short call, or could you point me to the right person on the
> team?

## A few notes on doing this well

- **Personalize the bracketed parts every time.** A message that's obviously
  copy-pasted (no company-specific detail) gets ignored. Spend 2 minutes on
  their profile/company before sending.
- **Pace yourself.** LinkedIn rate-limits connection requests, especially to
  people you don't know — sending many in a short burst can get requests
  restricted. 15-20 thoughtful ones a week beats 100 generic ones in a day.
- **Track who you've messaged.** Add a note in your job-search tracker (or a
  simple spreadsheet) so you don't message the same person twice or lose
  track of who replied.
- **Lead with your Little Extra Haircare result** (8M+ views, 5x Amazon
  search volume, 3x sales) — it's your strongest, most concrete proof point
  and works across almost every message type here.
